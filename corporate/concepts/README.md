# Third direction: decision memo

Wiseman Residential corporate site · concept round three · 11 September 2026

## Summary

You asked for a third direction that looks dramatically different from Tideline and The Plates while staying elegant, high end, clean, luxury and responsive, and you asked us to look outside the industry for it. We built four concept boards from hotel, resort, publishing and architecture references, each at 1440, full-page and 390, and put them in front of three independent judges: a creative director from a luxury hospitality agency, a renter on a phone in Los Angeles, and the front-end lead who would have to build the winner on the shared core. Each judge scored five criteria out of ten (distinct from the existing directions, luxury and elegance, renter clarity, feasibility on core/, responsive promise), so the ceiling is 150 across the three. All three judges ranked **Sanctuary** first (123 of 150). It is the only board that changes the first screen's grammar rather than restyling it, it is the most disciplined and the most buildable, and it measured clean at 360 and 1024. Its weakness is drama, and the judges were specific about which ideas from the other three boards fix that without breaking its register. We recommend building Sanctuary with three grafts: a renter-addressed headline (from Atelier), the reservation column on the building page (from Nocturne), and the measured caption under every photograph (from Atelier).

## What the judges said about each

### 1. Sanctuary (123)

Boards: [1440](sanctuary/board-1440.png) · [full page](sanctuary/board-full.png) · [390](sanctuary/board-390.png) · [live board](sanctuary/index.html)

**Strong.** "The only board of the four that changes the first screen's grammar": a daylight print inset inside the gutter under an opaque stone header with a centred wordmark, where every other board, and both existing directions, open on a full-bleed photograph with the headline low-left and a search line laid on it. Luxury scored highest of the four: "stillness, one accent, hairlines, museum labels", a building page that "reads like Aman's room page", and "the most disciplined board of the four and the one I would sign off to build tomorrow". The renter judge measured "the fastest Brentwood-two-bed path of the four and the most trustworthy building page": search inside the first screen at 360, three stacked buttons carrying the building's own number, the plan as a labelled card with its own Apply, office hours and the feed date directly beneath. Feasibility is the best: core untouched, a token-level re-skin, a 30-line crossfade, and "nothing overlapped in any frame" at 360 or 1024.

**Weak.** Distinctness is only moderate: the limestone is two RGB points from Tideline's secondary paper, "warm-neutral + serif + photograph is Tideline's territory", and it opens on the same photograph and the same tagline-as-headline that Golden uses. A client who wants dramatically different "may read it as Tideline with a mat around the picture". The centred wordmark, hairline booking bar and tracked caps are "the default Aman/Rosewood/Six Senses template"; the inset print, the salon hang and the clay dot are what keep it from being that template. Three fixes are required before build: Marcellus's inscriptional 1 reads as a lowercase l in counts and ranges; the label and body greys fail contrast on the stone (2.3:1 and 4.4:1 measured); and the headline is the company tagline rather than a renter sentence, which the phone drops entirely. The three primary buttons are identical ink slabs with no hierarchy.

### 2. Atelier (112)

Boards: [1440](atelier/board-1440.png) · [full page](atelier/board-full.png) · [390](atelier/board-390.png) · [live board](atelier/index.html)

**Strong.** "A genuine third register (no serif, no mono, a drawn grid, a condensed face)" and "the most original composition" of the four: the drawn twelve-column grid, the dimension line that draws itself, the title-block building page and the drawing-sheet footer. It has "the strongest renter page of the four": every caption is a measurable fact, price first, availability in signal, a specification table with office hours and the building's own line, a schedule of plans. "Your next address, to the square foot" is "the one headline of the four that speaks to a renter." Measured clean at 360 and 1024; the survey-orange Search is "the most findable button on any board". The dimension line is "excellent (cheap, fair-housing-safe, memorable)".

**Weak.** It fails the five-star test on register. Extra-condensed uppercase at 138px over the hero "is the voice of sportswear, event posters and coworking brands, not of a residence"; the orange Search "reads as a safety colour"; the column lines drawn through the photograph "look like a Figma layout grid someone forgot to hide". It "speaks to an architect, an investor or a construction client, not to someone choosing a home", closer to "a developer's capabilities brochure than a Rosewood". The amenity grid and spec table "read as a datasheet". One trust flaw: the hero label prints a unit count for Motor Tides that the data does not verify and the generator must never print. Grey buildings sink on the grey ground unless the build leans on the dusk and colour frames, and the grid alignment must survive 72 generated pages.

### 3. Nocturne (107)

Boards: [1440](nocturne/board-1440.png) · [full page](nocturne/board-full.png) · [390](nocturne/board-390.png) · [live board](nocturne/index.html)

**Strong.** "The most different site as a whole and the best pitch for dramatic": the only dark ground, a Didone, a horizontal reel, a sticky reservation column. The building page "with its sticky reservation column is the most refined renter layout of the four" and the "best CTA stack": homes available now in brass, Check availability & apply, "Rents from the leasing feed, not a quote", office hours and the feed date in one column. The search strip is the fullest of the four (Where, Bedrooms, Rent, More filters, Search). Bodoni over the sky "is elegant at 1440".

**Weak.** On the board it is "the one most likely to embarrass a five-star group": Bodoni numerals on near-black "have their hairlines eroded to nothing", and "those are the figures a renter reads". Brass on black is "the boutique-hotel template cliché ELEVATE explicitly warns against"; grain and vignette are "atmosphere as decoration". The reel dims 71 of 72 photographs at any moment, "the opposite of image-forward", and daylight stucco exteriors on velvet black "read as cut-outs pasted on". The first screen is the same skeleton as both existing directions, only inverted, and its hero is the exact photograph the retired Five Bedrooms direction opened on. It was the only board to fail the 360 probe: the facts strip overflowed, the floor-plan tabs clipped, the promised bottom bar never appeared. Dark maps force satellite tiles the client has not approved, and "Come home to the city at dusk" is "hospitality/nightlife copy for a landlord of Westside walk-ups".

### 4. Golden Hour (99)

Boards: [1440](golden/board-1440.png) · [full page](golden/board-full.png) · [390](golden/board-390.png) · [live board](golden/index.html)

**Strong.** "The most editorial display moment of the four": Bodoni at 8.8rem with the italic turn on "managed wisely." is "Cheval Blanc / Kinfolk", and the spreads are "magazine-grade". "Where do you want to live?" is "the best renter-facing search title of the four", the Brentwood spread directly answers the Brentwood seeker, and "Repairs and questions go to this office, not a call centre" is "the best trust line anywhere". The "Available now" and "Four bedrooms and up" saved searches surface the one checkable claim no Westside competitor can match.

**Weak.** "It is Tideline's silhouette in a different typeface": same warm light ground, serif display, full-bleed photograph, tagline as headline low-left over a scrim, bar riding the hero, editorial neighbourhood storytelling, dark footer, in the same order. Sand plus terracotta plus Didone italic "tips toward boho lifestyle brand or wedding stationery"; the staggered portfolio is "asymmetry as decoration". It is text-heavy against the image-forward rule (seven neighbourhood essays before the first building, a phone home about 8,300px long), and it was the one concept whose first screen fails the renter's door: at 360 the Search button sits well below the fold. Feasibility is the most fragile: the overlapping spread must survive 72 generated building pages with 500px galleries, and the rules must be carried verbatim into two templates or pages collide.

## Recommendation

**Build Sanctuary.** It won with all three judges on your own words (elegant, high end, clean, luxury, responsive), it is the only concept that changes what a visitor sees first rather than restyling it, it is the cheapest and safest to build on the shared core, and it is the only one that measured clean on a phone without corrections. Where it is short, on drama and on the renter voice, the other boards supply the fix.

Graft these three, in this order of importance:

1. **A headline that speaks to a renter (from Atelier).** Replace the tagline as the carved Marcellus line with a renter sentence in Sanctuary's voice, and demote "Los Angeles living, managed wisely." to the museum label or the sub-line. All three judges flagged the tagline-as-headline as a miss against your rule, and the phone board currently drops the only renter sentence it had. This also separates Sanctuary's opener from Golden's, which uses the same frame and the same line.

2. **The reservation column on the building page (from Nocturne).** A sticky column at 1060px and up, collapsing to Sanctuary's own bottom bar below: price first, "N homes available now" with the clay dot, then Apply as the solid ink button, Inquire in bone with a hairline, Call as a hairline with the number visible, and office hours and the feed date beneath, with the honesty line "Rents from the leasing feed, not a quote". Sanctuary's three identical ink slabs give a renter nothing to choose between and its facts row scrolls away; this is the clearest renter pattern on any board.

3. **The measured caption under every photograph (from Atelier).** Sanctuary's museum label gains a second line carrying a real fact from the feed (floor-area range, "studios to two bedrooms", and "72 buildings · 7 areas · 52 streets" under the hero print), drawn on reveal as a hairline from both ends, without Atelier's tick marks. It is cheap, fair-housing-safe, and gives Sanctuary "the one memorable mechanic beyond the breathing print that it currently lacks".

Two smaller borrowings ride along at no cost: the booking bar titled with Golden's "Where do you want to live?" and captioned "Results open on the search page, with a map", with the "Available now" and "Four bedrooms and up" saved searches beneath it; and Golden's trust line "Repairs and questions go to this office, not a call centre" under the office hours on every building page.

Sanctuary fixes the build must carry regardless: set counts, ranges and rents in Jost tabular and keep Marcellus for words (so "13" never reads as "l3"); raise the two grey tokens until 11px labels and 15px body pass 4.5:1 on the stone; set the from-rent in the hang at 15px or larger rather than 11px caps; enforce the four-uses-a-page budget on clay in the templates; derive every hung frame's width from the photograph's native size so 500px thumbnails are never upscaled past about 1.2x; and re-verify the two-row booking bar inside the first screen at 360x740. One choice to make with your eyes open: Sanctuary's hero is inset inside the gutter, a deliberate departure from the "full-bleed" wording in the brief. The print still fills the viewport height and 91% of its width; if you want the bleed, it can bleed on the home only while every other photograph stays inset.

## What stays the same in every option

Every one of the four concepts is a skin over the same working site. Choosing between them changes tokens, one stylesheet, one script and two page templates, and nothing below:

- **Search.** The Zillow-style filter bar with its dropdown popovers and the More-filters dialog (core/search.js and search.css), spliced into every direction by scripts/gen_search.py. The home search always submits to the results page (search.html) with a map; the search-page header stays compact.
- **Map.** Leaflet over OpenStreetMap colour tiles (core/map.js and map.css), plain dot pins with no tails, numbers or count bubbles, and price pills on the results page. Each direction sets only the map tint and pin colour.
- **Rails.** Arrow-driven horizontal rails with hidden scrollbars (core/rail.js and rail.css).
- **Header and page behaviour.** The header never retracts, shares one gutter with the content at every width, and there are no blank bands under sticky bars; reveals, favourites and typeahead come from core/core.js and base.css.
- **Building and area pages.** scripts/build_site.py generates all 72 building pages and 7 area pages from the chosen direction's templates. Every building page carries its photographs, floor plans, amenity icons, office hours, the building's own leasing line and RentCafe link, and primary Apply, Inquire and Call buttons. No property is numbered anywhere.
- **Editorial pages.** Company, residents and contact pages come from scripts/gen_editorial.py.
- **Data and facts.** data/wiseman.json (72 buildings with galleries, plans, amenities and coordinates), data/areas.json for neighbourhood copy, data/artdirection.json for which frames survive at large sizes, and the twenty 1800px photographs in assets/img. FACT-CHECK.md is binding in every option: no founding year or years-in-business claim, no round-number boasts, no awards, never a rent figure for Motor Tides, fair-housing-safe copy, Motor Tides placed in Palms, minutes from Culver City.
- **Responsive floor.** Nothing overlaps at any width from 360 to 1366, no single-word last lines in headings, fonts from Google Fonts only, vanilla HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no framework.
